# Cheap custom inline illustrations as an explanatory advantage (hypothesis)

## What was observed / claimed

The maintainer reports a prior, undated learning session in which he was learning about transistors with an AI agent that drew small custom illustrations inline as it explained, and recalls the experience as notably good — the pictures, arriving as part of the explanation, helped the concepts land. From that he advances a hypothesis: **cheap, custom, purpose-drawn illustrations are a key explanatory advantage the agent has**, and the methodology should make reaching for one a first-class move rather than a teaching-mode footnote.

The make-or-break property he names is **non-interruption**: for an illustration to help while teaching it must arrive *with* the prose, mid-explanation — the way a whiteboard sketch appears while someone is still talking — not as a slow, separate "generating an illustration" step that breaks the flow.

This is explicitly offered as an idea to try, not as a validated result.

## Specific claims

- In at least one self-observed learning session, inline custom illustrations made a technical concept (transistors) land better for the maintainer.
- The value depends on the illustration being fast, simple, and non-interrupting — composed as part of the explanation, not a detour.
- The capability is general enough to lift out of teaching mode into a cross-cutting explanatory tool (usable in `learn`, at a gate, in a `gather` landscape, in conversation).

## Implications (suggested, not concluded)

- Bears on **A16** (building the second output): illustration is a device for the *build* half of the teach→gate loop — helping understanding form — which is the capacity the methodology is trying to grow.
- Bears on **A13** as a risk, not a benefit: a fluent picture can manufacture the *feeling* of understanding the gate later finds hollow (the cognitive-offloading failure mode). An illustration must lay out the terrain, never carry the conclusion the researcher should reach.
- Bears on **Q13.2** (does a dedicated teaching mode build understanding better): the inline-visualization clause is one of G27's `speculative` strands; realizing it as the `illustrate` skill makes the clause exercisable in real use, so first-use friction/reinforcement becomes evidence on it.
- *Might* support shipping an `illustrate` skill at status `testing`. (Taken up as note 2 of update 0007; the maintainer's call is to ship it to the friendly-pilot audience now as the trial.)

## Notes / caveats

- Anecdotal: one recalled self-observation plus a design hypothesis, no measurement and no second subject. This is the thin basis the `illustrate` trial rides on, and the status (`testing`, clause stays `speculative`) reflects that.
- The strongest counter-risk is the A13/offloading one above; the skill's hard lines (the picture never carries the conclusion; it never stamps) are written against it, but whether they hold is exactly what the trial watches.
- Surface-dependent: true inline rendering exists only on some surfaces (the Claude app); elsewhere the illustration degrades to a saved-and-linked file, which is a weaker version of the experience the hypothesis is about.
